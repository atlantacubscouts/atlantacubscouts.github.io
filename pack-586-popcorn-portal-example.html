Storefront sales

A storefront is like a lemonade stand for popcorn. Scouts staff a table outside a local business and greet customers coming in and out.

What families should expect

  • Shifts run for two hours, with two Scouts per booth so nobody sells alone.
  • A parent stays on site for the entire shift. Scouts are never left unattended.
  • The Pack provides the table, popcorn, and Pack 586 banner.
  • The first-shift parent helps set up. The last-shift parent helps take down and return materials.
  • Popcorn Pack Leaders move supplies and inventory between families.

Locations and flow

  • Kroger has two booths operating at the same time: one at the store entrance and one facing the Beltline.
  • Intown Ace Hardware is a single booth with a shorter, calmer schedule.
  • Lions, Tigers, and Wolves are intentionally favored for the Ace dates.
  • If your home-den date does not work, claim an open slot on another date after filling your own den’s day first.

Quick answers

Open a question for the short version. The detailed instructions above are still the source of truth for this example.

What should we do first?
Download Trail’s End, create or update the Scout’s account with unit code CF4CM, complete the photo and bio, and share the Scout’s personal sales page.
Do we have to sell at a storefront?
No. Online sales are strongly encouraged because they are simple and can reach family and friends anywhere. One storefront shift is another way to help reach the $250 goal.
What does a family bring to a storefront?
A parent, the Scout, and a willingness to help for the full shift. The Pack provides the table, popcorn, and banner. First and last shifts also include setup or takedown responsibilities.
Can a Scout from another den join a date?
Yes. If the home-den date does not work, families may claim an open slot on another date. Families with Scouts in different dens can coordinate so the children sell together.
Where do I sign up?
Use the Trail’s End app, open the Pack 586 storefront schedule, and claim the shift that works for your family. If the app is difficult to use, contact Chris Bower.
